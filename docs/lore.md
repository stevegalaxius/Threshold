# Setting and lore

Curated from a brainstormed setting export, checked against the game's
factions, outlets, stations and yards. Where the export used different names
or details from the game (outlet names and editors, yard names, which Mars moon
holds which works, and so on), the game's version is used and the export's is
discarded without comment. Writing reference only; no game data changes.

## The premise

Non-relativistic travel; mass drivers fire ships between planets. Four major
powers (the Estates, Zhuhai, the Diwan, Jove) sit in a 200-year equilibrium
that is mutual dependency, not peace. Each runs on a belief that can't be
disproved, and its news outlet repeats it. The outer system is independents,
company towns and drifters (Ceres Concern, Milliways, Bowman Atmospherics).

## The powers

**The Estates (Earth, Venus, Mercury).** Hereditary houses plus the remnant UN.
Venus's Garden is habitable only through constant machine maintenance, and the
maintenance contracts are the economy, handed out by rank: the Court, the
Season (rank is set in public), the Chancery (awards contracts). Status
matters more than power. Mercury is the ugly automated foundry that makes the
Garden possible and goes unmentioned; its technicians are free but redundant.
Ships are elegant, fragile and maintenance-hungry.

**Zhuhai (Mars).** An industrial state ruled by the Mandate, a rotating
council of founding conglomerates, built to prevent domination and so built to
deadlock. Creed: "cast the mountains into sea; boil the sea into heaven."
Class names: Contract-Bearers, Dust-Workers, Ring-Rats. Workers are bound but
essential, the mirror of Mercury's free but redundant technicians; neither
side can prove the other wrong. The poor live in the cramped spokes, the rich
on a sunlit tier, and colonists gaze back at Earth from the Home-Gazing
Terrace. Ships are heavy and overbuilt.

**Jove, the Olympian Accord (Jupiter system).** A federation of station-states
with no capital and no head of state: a rotating council that deadlocks, a
shared navy that employs more people than it protects, and docking law nobody
understands and everybody cites. It holds because a paying customer is cheaper
than a war. Kyrakos is the customs house and dispute court. Each Galilean moon
has a role: Io sulfur and silicates, Europa water and salt, Ganymede ammonia
and fertilizer, Callisto logistics and records. Ships are modular and
repairable.

**The Diwan of Ephraim (Saturn).** A small, closed dynasty holding the
birthright and chemistry of a buried world: the petrostate that sells the
system just enough to keep everyone dependent. Ephraim is the ruling tribe
(Titan); Manasseh the working tribe (Enceladus). The Trust holds the
hydrocarbon reserves as a sacred stewardship; the Qibla Fund is its
speculative arm. The House is older and stranger than its public faith. Its
domestic Hebrew press argues with the government and is never translated.
Ships are sealed, precise and not for sale.

## Secondary places

- **Ceres Concern**: the Belt's nitrate and explosives works, named for the
  goddess of agriculture and for the worry that comes with what it makes.
- **Ring Station**: built jointly by the Estates and Zhuhai as a gesture of
  reconciliation, run by a commission that agrees on nothing, now the Belt's
  largest port. The one place both powers' favour matters at once.
- **Bowman Atmospherics (Uranus)**: a chartered company town of shareholder
  families, plain, practical and suspicious of outsiders; crews are contractors
  on fixed rotations.
- **Triton**: the last stop before the lanes run out; bonded warehouses and
  cold storage for traders and people who don't want to be found.
- **Freeports** (Milliways, the Shoals, Cordelia): neutral ground where anyone
  can dock if they leave their guns at the door; the grey market lives here.
- **Thule Prime (Luna)**: settled by the descendants of an old ideology,
  isolated for centuries, now a shabby remnant nobody takes seriously.
  Also on Luna: the far-side Clavius observatories (the system's deep-space
  tracking network) and an Estates legation that works as a tripwire in a
  nice suit.
- **The Hollow**: a network of abandoned Belt stations inhabited by people who
  left the economy for good; nothing there can be owned. A natural fit for a
  discoverable hidden site (`data/sites.lua`).

## Trade

Jupiter clears the major contracts, Ring Station handles smaller spot deals,
and the freeports handle what appears on no exchange. Only the Belt and Jovian
space let a newcomer buy into the commodity trade; elsewhere you work for
someone. This matches the game's hub-to-hub logistics.

## Timeline

Venus is terraformed and the Estates are born; Mars declares independence and
fights over the Ladder; the Jovian stations fight their wars and sign the
Accord; the Diwan secedes from Jove and a tariff war settles into cold peace;
the Long Equilibrium follows. The export's year counts don't agree with each
other, so only the order is kept until a calendar is chosen.

  fanatic group. It doesn't fit and is dropped.
- **Reactor tiers (chemical / fission / fusion)**: a systems design question,
  not lore.
